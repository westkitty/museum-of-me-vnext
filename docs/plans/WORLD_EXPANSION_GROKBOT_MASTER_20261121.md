## 2. Frozen creative identity and experience
The museum starts as an authored art/portfolio space. The visitor initially sees an enormous living wooded island, not an arcade hub. Secret gateways feel physically discovered. Each pocket-world has a memorable entrance, distinct traversal mechanic, short playable arc and safe return. Typical target 3–7 minutes of authored core route, with optional exploration outside the critical path. Not empty panoramas, not menus disguised as worlds.
AWE moments (design hypotheses, not human-verified emotion): the ordinary greenhouse unfolds into a leaf-sized universe; the ruined observatory stops a meteor fall as the visitor stops; the rooftop pad launches toward two visible destinations; the lighthouse lens carries the visitor into an impossible beam puzzle; the raindrop falls; Dexter stands on a wild cliff as a grudging adventurer. Every spectacle must have player agency and gameplay or meaningful discovery immediately afterward.
No spiders or spider-like creature substitutions. Avoid copying copyrighted Nintendo, Portal, or SUPERHOT maps, music, art, character/weapon designs or text; borrow abstract gameplay patterns, author original dressing.
