- Versioned migration: placement schema version 2 raises the record cap from 256 to 4,096 with the same record shape. Version-1 files are still accepted (with the 256 cap enforced), migrated in memory, and reported via `migratedFrom`; unknown or newer versions are refused and their on-disk bytes are preserved. The local save body limit rose from 256 KiB to 4 MiB. `tests/workshop-schema-migration.test.ts` covers v1 acceptance, cap enforcement, 2,000 distributed records through museum conservation, byte-stable round trip, downgrade and refusal, and a real 2,000-record save over a v1 file with the backup verified. Accepting 2,000 records does not yet render them efficiently: every saved record still builds a live root.
- Bounded placement residency: manifests of 384 placements or fewer keep every placement live (unchanged behaviour). Larger manifests keep only the nearest active-world placements within 60 m of the camera live, capped at 384, re-evaluated when the camera moves 4 m or the world changes (one distance check per frame otherwise; no new render loop). Dormant placements remain saved records: capture/save is lossless, `getObject` materializes on demand, and the editor pins its selection. Roots moved by an uncaptured live edit, and solid catalog models whose bounds are not yet known, are never unloaded. Solid collision for dormant placements is derived from cached per-shape mesh bounds and the record transform, matching `Box3.setFromObject` on the live root, so navigation does not depend on visual residency. `tests/workshop-placement-residency.test.ts` (6 tests) covers 2,000 records (189 live at the test focus, about 59 ms CPU setup in Node — not GPU or frame time), eviction disposal, 2,000 solid colliders with dormant/live bounds equality, pin and live-edit protection, and dormant edit/removal collision updates. It does not exercise disk save/reload of 2,000 records (covered by the unit save test). Not yet: device frame-time measurements.

- Clear Walkways (mission item 5): `ClearWalkways.ts` derives protected walkways from the v2 index — every canonical route leg, both curved stair runs at full width, each of the 35 exhibit doorway-to-visitor-position approaches, installation interaction points, and the entrance threshold inside and out — each kept 1.5 m clear. Detection is deterministic: an authored placement whose bounds enter a walkway between walkable floor height and head height is FAIL (solid), WARNING (non-solid) or UNKNOWN (bounds not yet known); with no runtime bounds the status is UNKNOWN, never PASS. Proposals are the smallest of 16 directions × 0.25–3 m moves that clear every walkway with margin, pass museum conservation, and avoid exhibit bounds, installation footprints and other placements; locked or unknown-bounds placements are never moved and nothing is ever deleted. The Workshop panel lists findings with Preview (green ghost box, object unmoved), Approve (one undoable history step, then rescan) and Reject (now remembered by Curator's Memory). Note: halls are already protected by conservation, so realistic obstructions occur at bays, approaches and thresholds. Tests: 7 unit tests and a real Chromium journey (detect FAIL, preview ghost, approve, PASS, undo restores position) passed locally. Limits: axis-aligned bounds, geometric not walked traversal, static architecture not assessed.
- Curator's Memory (mission item 6): version-2 manifests carry an optional `curator` field holding locks, the latest curator decision per object and suggestion kind (approved, rejected or user-override, with position and ISO time), and deliberately empty XZ regions per world. It is validated (locks and decisions must reference existing objects; at most 4,096 decisions and 256 regions), canonicalized, omitted when empty, saved through the normal local save and backup path, and carried through record edits and removal; a v1 downgrade refuses non-empty memory instead of dropping it. Clear Walkways never moves locked objects, does not re-propose a move the curator rejected or overrode while the object stays at that position (moving it again reopens the question), and never proposes a destination inside an empty region. In the Workshop, Reject and Approve are recorded in the same undoable history step as their effect; hand moves of an object with a walkway decision record a user override; each finding row and the new Curator's memory panel can lock or unlock; the panel can mark the selection's footprint plus 0.5 m as deliberately empty and list and remove regions. Tests: 6 Curator's Memory unit tests (schema validation, refusal of malformed memory, one decision per kind, v1 downgrade refusal, edit/delete/undo, real local save and reload), 4 Clear Walkways memory tests, and the Clear Walkways Chromium journey extended to reject (persisted decision, suggestion suppressed, object unmoved) and lock (persisted). Not yet: named checkpoints, and memory for kinds other than walkway moves (added when Auto-Curate exists).

- Exhibit Kits (mission item 8): version-2 manifests carry optional `kits`. Each kit has a parent transform (floor position plus yaw), a template, and slot-to-record membership. Member records keep world transforms, so collision, residency, conservation, walkways and health need no special cases. Each member's local transform is derived from the kit origin, so there is one source of truth. Validation refuses unknown members, a record in two kits, cross-world members, kit/record id clashes, unknown templates and extra fields; the v1 downgrade refuses non-empty kits. `ExhibitKits.ts` provides two examples built only from the bundled licensed library, with nothing stacked: Archive Research Station (desk, chair, two shelves, document table) and Observatory Workstation (telescope, desk, chair, computer, star-chart shelf). It supports place, move/rotate as a parent (overrides preserved), follow-member (an edit of one part moves the kit, yaw only), override detection, reset or re-create one slot, duplicate (fresh ids, overrides and finishes kept), ungroup and delete (locks and decisions of deleted members pruned). Removing a single member through the normal path drops its membership. In the Workshop: place a kit in view, a "Move as kit" toggle (off makes an edit an override of that part), and Copy whole kit, Reset this part, Ungroup and Delete kit, each one undoable step. Tests: 8 unit tests (examples valid and conservation-safe, parent move/rotate with exact quarter-turn positions, follow-member, reset/re-create, duplicate, invalid kits and downgrade refusal, real local save and reload with overrides, runtime undo/removal/deletion with resource disposal) and a Chromium journey (move as kit, override then reset, duplicate and undo, delete releasing every part and undo). Not yet: a dedicated parent gizmo; artistic review of the template layouts in place.
- Auto-Curate & Clear (mission item 10): `AutoCurate.ts` produces deterministic suggestions only, in three modes: clear (Clear Walkways moves, so locks, standing rejections and empty regions apply), furnish (gallery benches), and both (benches avoid where moved objects will land). Density caps benches per exhibit zone: Minimal one, Restrained one per three exhibits, Rich one per exhibit. Scope is the whole museum or any single zone. A bench is proposed only on a 0.5 m grid inside its exhibit's own bay, nearest the visitor position, facing the exhibit, and only where it: passes museum conservation (so exhibit reading zones, installation footprints and circulation stay free); clears every interior walkway with a 0.25 m margin; is at least 3 m from the display anchor and 4 m from other benches; is outside other exhibits' bays (3D), installations, other placements and curator empty areas; does not overlap a wall envelope; and stands on a floor at all four corners. Without the interior collision world it suggests nothing. Approval applies the chosen suggestions as one undoable step and records an approved decision (new `furnish-add` decision kind); rejection is recorded and that bench is never proposed again. In the Workshop: mode, density and scope selectors, Suggest, Preview (ghost box, nothing changes), Approve, Reject and Approve all shown. Tests: 6 unit tests against the real built museum collision world (no collision means no suggestions; every Rich bench conservation-valid, walkway-clear, inside its bay, 3 m from the display and 4 m apart, and the whole set a valid manifest; Minimal < Restrained < Rich with at most one per zone at Minimal; scope; determinism; approve/reject memory; empty areas; clear and combined modes with locks) and a Chromium journey (furnish North wing, preview leaves the manifest unchanged, approve adds exactly that bench and remembers it, reject is remembered and reported, two undos restore the original). Limits: non-colliding exhibit dressing is not detected (preview before approving); benches only; walls by axis-aligned envelope; whole-museum furnishing takes about 1.3 s in Node.
